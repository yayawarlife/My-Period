    How your cycle works

    A menstrual cycle runs from the first day of one period to the day before the next. Hormones shift through four phases along the way. Here’s what each term in this app means.

    MenstrualFollicularOvulationLuteal

    Menstrual phase

    Roughly days 1–5

    Your period. If no pregnancy happened in the last cycle, levels of the hormones estrogen and progesterone fall, and the lining of the uterus sheds as menstrual bleeding.

    Many people notice cramps, lower back pain, tiredness or headaches during these days.

    Follicular phase

    From day 1 until ovulation, roughly days 1–13

    Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) prompts the ovaries to grow several follicles, small sacs that each hold an egg. One becomes dominant. Rising estrogen rebuilds the uterine lining.

    The follicular phase technically starts on day 1, so it overlaps with your period. In this app’s calendar, the green follicular color starts after your period ends, so the two are easy to tell apart. This phase is the one that varies most in length, which is why cycles can be longer or shorter from month to month.

    Ovulation

    Around day 14 of a 28-day cycle

    A surge of luteinizing hormone (LH) makes the dominant follicle release its egg. The egg can be fertilized for about 12–24 hours.

    Ovulation usually happens about 14 days before the next period, not 14 days after the last one. So in a 32-day cycle it’s closer to day 18. The app highlights the estimated ovulation day plus the day before and after, because the exact day is hard to pin down from dates alone.

    Fertile window

    About 5 days before ovulation through the day after

    Sperm can survive in the body for up to 5 days, so pregnancy is possible from several days before ovulation until about a day after. These days are marked with a small dot on the calendar.

    Luteal phase

    From after ovulation until your next period, usually 12–14 days

    The empty follicle becomes the corpus luteum and releases progesterone, which keeps the uterine lining ready for a possible pregnancy. If no pregnancy happens, progesterone drops and the next period starts.

    Premenstrual symptoms, such as bloating, breast tenderness, mood changes or cravings, tend to show up late in this phase.

    Cycle length

    Usually 21–35 days for adults

    The number of days from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. This app shows the average of your last 6 cycles. Until you’ve logged two periods, it assumes 28 days.

    Period length

    Usually 2–7 days

    How many days of bleeding each period lasts. The app uses the average of your last 6 logged periods.

    How the predictions work

    • Next period = start of your last period + your average cycle length.
    • Ovulation day = your average cycle length minus 14. That’s day 14 in a 28-day cycle, or day 18 in a 32-day cycle.
    • Fertile window = 5 days before ovulation through 1 day after.
    • Days logged within two days of each other count as one period.
    • Gaps shorter than 15 days or longer than 60 days are left out of the average, since they’re usually a missed log.

    When to talk to a doctor

    • Your cycles are regularly shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days.
    • Your period lasts longer than 7 days, or soaks through a pad or tampon every hour or two.
    • You’ve missed three or more periods in a row and aren’t pregnant.
    • Period pain stops you from doing your usual activities.
    • You bleed between periods or after sex.

    This page is general information, not medical advice. Everyone’s cycle is different, and a doctor can help with anything that feels unusual for you.
